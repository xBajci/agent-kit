# Research grounding

Knowledge snapshot: 2026-10-02. Use these primary publisher sources. Distinguish design guidance, empirical findings, and technical interaction guidance. Verify precise claims and current APIs/standards when needed. Do not claim fresh browsing unless performed.

1. **NN/g — Progressive Disclosure (2006)**
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/
   Use for essential versus specialized options, clear labels, prioritization, and progressive versus staged disclosure. Treat shallow hierarchy as a heuristic, not an absolute two-level limit.

2. **NN/g — Accordions on Desktop (2023)**
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-on-desktop/
   Use for shorter pages versus hidden content/interaction cost, simultaneous comparison, and users needing most sections.

3. **NN/g — Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics (2016)**
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hidden-navigation-methodology/
   Treat the study of 179 participants and six sites as navigation evidence under tested conditions. Do not transfer percentages to accordions/advanced settings. Distinguish navigation use, content discovery, and task success.

4. **GOV.UK — Details and Accordion**
   https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/details/
   https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/accordion/
   Use for optional content, descriptive triggers, headings/anchor-link alternatives, comparison, and unnecessary nesting. Treat as component guidance, not universal effect sizes.

5. **GOV.UK — Conditional questions**
   https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/radios/
   https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2021/09/21/an-update-on-the-accessibility-of-conditionally-revealed-questions/
   Use for simple follow-up fields and observed difficulties with complex reveals for assistive-technology users. Consult current guidance for known issues; historical investigation does not certify every implementation.

6. **Baymard — Applied Filters Overview**
   https://baymard.com/research-articles/how-to-design-applied-filters
   Use for applied-value context/removal controls outside filter panels. Count-only summaries performed poorly in described ecommerce testing. Transfer to admin tables is an inference to validate.

7. **W3C WAI-ARIA APG — Disclosure Pattern**
   https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/
   https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/
   Use for custom button semantics, expanded state, optional controls relationship, and Enter/Space. Following APG alone does not establish full WCAG conformance or usability.

Avoid arbitrary option limits based on Hick's law or working-memory folklore. Do not promise conversion gains, percentage reductions in cognitive load, or universal task-time benefits. Mark unsupported local proposals as hypotheses.
